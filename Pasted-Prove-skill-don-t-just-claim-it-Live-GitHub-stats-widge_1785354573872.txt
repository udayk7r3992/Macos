Hardware realism
Notch with a functioning camera dot — tiny dark circle with a subtle green light-catch when "active" (e.g. flickers briefly during boot, like a real camera indicator)
Aluminum bezel with realistic gradient — not flat black, a subtle brushed-metal gradient with soft highlight along the top edge
Laptop base/keyboard deck hint — a sliver of keyboard-deck visible below the hinge, even just a gradient bar, sells "this is a device" instantly
Screen glare/reflection overlay — a very faint diagonal white gradient at low opacity over the whole screen, like light hitting glass
Rounded screen corners matching real MacBook radius — screens aren't sharp-cornered, subtle rounding matters
Subtle screen vignette — very slight darkening at the edges, mimics how displays actually look in photos
macOS software realism (beyond what you have)
Real menu bar app icons on the right — WiFi, Bluetooth, battery percentage, Spotlight icon — not just clock/control center
Traffic light hover behavior — icons (×, −, ⤢) should only appear ON HOVER, not always visible — that's exactly how real macOS works and most portfolio clones get this wrong
Window shadow depth — real macOS windows cast a soft, large-radius shadow; if yours is a tight small shadow it reads as "flat design" not "OS"
Cursor changes — resize cursors at window edges, pointer vs default states
Menu bar transparency reacting to wallpaper — real macOS menu bar shows a frosted blur of whatever's behind it; if yours is a flat color it'll look fake by comparison
Motion realism
Genie effect on minimize — windows should shrink toward the dock, not just fade out
Dock magnification on hover — icons near the cursor scale up smoothly, neighbors scale slightly too (real macOS dock physics)
Spring-based window open animation — scale from 0.9→1 with slight overshoot, not a linear fade

Depth and materials
True vibrancy/frosted glass — layered blur with a subtle noise/grain texture on translucent surfaces (menu bar, dock, Control Center); flat backdrop-blur alone looks synthetic, real macOS blur has texture
Dynamic wallpaper-aware tinting — dock/menu bar pick up a faint color tint from the current wallpaper, like real macOS vibrancy does
Proper light/dark shadow physics — shadows should shift subtly warmer/cooler and softer depending on dark/light mode, not just opacity changes
Icon depth — dock icons with a subtle bottom highlight/bevel instead of flat emoji-in-a-box
Micro-interactions nobody thinks to add
Dock bounce on app launch — icon bounces once when you open a window from it (not just on hover)
Window snapping — drag a window to the screen edge and it snaps to half-screen, like macOS/Windows tiling
Right-click context menus — on desktop, dock icons, and windows — real OS feel is defined as much by what responds to right-click as left
Drag-to-select on desktop — click-drag on empty desktop draws a selection rectangle, even if it doesn't do anything yet
Double-click title bar to maximize — small but everyone tries it instinctively
Inertial scroll — momentum/easing on scrollable content instead of instant-stop scroll
System-level touches
Battery indicator that's actually stateful — starts at some %, slowly ticks down over the session, maybe charges if you "plug in" via a fake menu toggle
Do Not Disturb toggle — actually suppresses your notification banner when on
Screenshot tool — Cmd+Shift+4-style drag-to-capture that generates a real downloadable PNG of the desktop (this one is genuinely impressive and demonstrates real skill)
Multiple monitor simulation — a small "external display" toggle that repositions the dock, extremely advanced but a huge wow-factor if you get there eventually
Content polish that reinforces realism
